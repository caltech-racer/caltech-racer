Overview

A two-term course that brings the Caltech Racer project into the classroom. You'll develop a high-performance autonomous racing system under uncertainty, from controllers in simulation to a race on physical hardware.

Caltech Racer is the Autonomous Robotics and Controls Lab's (ARCL) entry in the Indy Autonomous Challenge. The course uses the same tools as the research team: a high-fidelity racing simulator built on Assetto Corsa, shared performance benchmarks, and the same racing format used on the track. Students working on advanced projects can also use selected data and sensor recordings from Caltech's AV-24 racecar.

Winter term

A: Planning and control in simulation

Teams develop racing trajectories and implement classical, optimization-based and learning-based controllers, then compare lap time, tracking accuracy, safety, computational efficiency and robustness on shared benchmarks.

Final project: a software race in simulation
Spring term

B: Localization, perception and hardware

Teams run their Winter controllers, or provided baselines, on physical racing hardware, integrating localization, track perception, state estimation and closed-loop control with real sensors.

Final project: a race on physical hardware

What you'll learn

  • Make principled design trade-offs between speed, safety and robustness
  • Diagnose control failures when a vehicle operates near its physical limits
  • Debug an autonomy stack end to end, from sensors to actuation
  • Measure the gap between simulation and the real world: sensor noise, latency and model mismatch
  • Design reproducible experiments and evaluate performance at the system level

Policies

Teamwork and collaboration

Labs and races are done in teams. You may discuss approaches with other teams, but each team's code and write-ups must be its own. Each team member submits a short peer evaluation at the end of the term.

Hardware and lab safety (Spring)

Complete the lab safety briefing before working with the racing hardware. Vehicles run only with course staff present, in the designated test area, with an emergency stop within reach.

Late work

Each team has a set number of late days per term for labs. Race days can't be moved, so plan your final testing accordingly.

Use of AI tools

You may use AI assistants to understand concepts and debug code. Disclose any substantial use in your write-up, and be ready to explain every part of your team's code.

Accommodations

If you have accommodations through Caltech Student Wellness Services, email the course staff in the first two weeks so we can plan for labs and race days.
